## Present the work, not just the code

A project only helps your career if others can understand it quickly. For each portfolio project, write a short,
honest **case study** (in the README and/or a blog post):

- **The problem** and who it is for.
- **Your approach** and the key decisions (and trade-offs — why this model, this retrieval, this metric).
- **The results**: the evaluation, with the baseline and limitations (lesson 3) — honest numbers, not cherry-picked
  demos.
- **What you learned** and what you would do next.

Make it easy to see it work: a clear README with run steps, and where feasible a hosted demo (e.g. Hugging Face
Spaces) or screenshots/a short video. Pin your best two or three projects on your GitHub profile; depth on a few beats
a pile of half-finished repos.

## Choose a certification by goal

Certifications signal baseline knowledge and help with interviews and some roles; they complement, not replace, a
portfolio. Choose by **where you want to go**, then verify the details:

- **AI/ML engineering** — foundational GenAI/LLM certs and cloud ML-engineer certifications; the **LangChain LCAE**
  and **NVIDIA agentic-AI** credentials for agent work (Stage 15).
- **Cloud/MLOps** — the cloud provider's ML-engineer and associate certifications (Stage 12).
- **Security** — the Cybersecurity roadmap's path (Security+, CySA+, CEH/PenTest+), if that is your direction.

Study Hub's **Certifications area** lists these with concepts, which Study Hub courses prepare for them, and verified
exam facts. Always confirm the current **cost, format and content on the provider's official page** before committing
— these change, and this course does not hard-code them.

## Make a realistic plan

- **Match the level** — start with a foundational/associate cert; do not jump to an expert exam cold.
- **Map the exam objectives** to the courses and projects you have done; study the gaps, not what you already know.
- **Use official and free materials** — the provider's exam guide and practice questions, and the free resources each
  certification entry links.
- **Practise under exam conditions** and build hands-on (a cert backed by a real project is far more convincing than
  the badge alone).
- **Schedule it** — a date turns "someday" into preparation.

## The through-line

You have gone from Python fundamentals to building, evaluating, serving and governing AI systems — and, if you took
the Cybersecurity roadmap, from IT fundamentals to authorised offensive and defensive security. The capstone ties it
together into something you can **show and stand behind**, and the certification marks the milestone. Keep the habits
that run through every stage: build the simplest thing that works, measure honestly, keep it reproducible and secure,
and be straight about what it does and does not do. That is what makes an engineer, with or without the badge.
